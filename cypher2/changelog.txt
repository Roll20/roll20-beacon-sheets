Cypher System 2 Character Sheet - Changelog
============================================

2026-11-03
- Token bars can now be pointed at most of the sheet instead of just the three
  Pools. Open a token's settings, pick bar 1, 2 or 3, and the list of values now
  includes your minor, moderate and major wounds as three separate bars, the
  same three for a shield if you carry one, the Luck Pool, Stress points, where
  you are on the damage track, how many recovery rolls you have left, how many
  cyphers you are carrying against your limit, XP, Story XP, resource points,
  your tier, your Effort and superhero rank.
- Most of those bars can be typed into, and the number goes back onto the sheet.
  Wounds, the shield, Luck, Stress, the damage track and the three XP-style
  counters all work that way, the way the Pools already did. Clearing a bubble
  or typing something that is not a number changes nothing, so a stray keystroke
  in the middle of a fight cannot wipe a value.
- A few are deliberately read-only, because a bar is a bad place to change them.
  Tier and Effort are read-only so a mis-drag cannot quietly re-rank your
  character. Recovery rolls are read-only because the slots are in order and a
  single number cannot say which rest you spent. Cyphers carried counts your
  rows, so it changes when you add or remove a cypher.
- A wound bar fills up as you get hurt, the opposite of a Pool bar, because it
  is counting the wounds you have taken rather than what you have left.
- A value your character does not have reads as blank rather than as zero. A
  shield you are not carrying, a Luck Pool you do not have, Stress or the damage
  track at a table that does not use them, and a rank outside the superhero
  genre all show nothing at all.
- Anything you put on a bar can be read on the map by anyone allowed to see that
  bar. That has always been true of Health on an NPC, and it is worth knowing now
  that wounds, Stress and the damage track can go there too.

2026-10-30
- A character file that arrives with a rule switched off but numbers still in it
  now keeps those numbers when you switch the rule on. That covers Stress points
  and the damage track step. Before, turning the rule on wrote fresh starting
  values over whatever the file carried, and there was no way to keep it. Because
  nothing is thrown away any more, turning a rule on no longer asks twice.
  Switching one off still throws its numbers away and still asks twice.
- Exporting a character no longer refuses over a damaged value in the file it was
  built from. A shield, pools, wounds, advancement or armor modifiers saved in the
  wrong shape used to stop the whole export at that field.
- A character whose list rows carry a damaged position now loads. One case could
  silently drop a row, another could leave two rows fighting over one slot, and a
  third could stall the sheet for over a minute per list. Rows now load in the
  order their positions give, with anything unreadable last.
- A Luck Pool whose name is blank in the file now reads "Luck" on the panel and on
  the chat card. Before, the panel heading printed nothing and the card read
  "Spent 3 - 1 Might, 2 " with nothing after it.
- The d20 on the pool cards is taller, so it is easier to hit on a touch screen.
  It matches the minimum size the cog and the other small controls already used.

2026-10-29
- The Attacks tab has a Roll initiative button above the list. Initiative is a
  Speed task, so you could always roll it from the Speed pool, but you had to
  know that and find it on the other side of the sheet in the middle of a fight.
  The button opens the usual roller with Speed already chosen, and the card it
  posts to chat says Initiative. You can still change the pool and pick a skill
  the way you would for any other task.
- The guided roller has a Situational hindrance counter, beside Assets and
  Effort. It starts at 0 and leaving it alone changes nothing, so it works the
  way Assets do. When the GM says a task is hindered two steps, set it to 2 and
  both the preview and the chat card say so. It goes up to 10 and resets every
  time the roller opens. The Magnus Archives Stress hindrances are unchanged and
  still sit in their own group.
- On a creature sheet, the Description's "Share description" button is now a
  small send-to-chat icon sitting with the description itself, matching the
  icons the creature's combat actions already use. It posts exactly what it
  posted before.
- Importing a character file written for a schema version this sheet does not
  read used to say "Schema version: must be 2" directly beneath a hint saying
  the sheet accepts versions 2 and 3. It now names both versions and the one the
  file claims.
- Dragging a compendium page that Roll20 has not filed under a category used to
  refuse with "No Category pages can't be added to a sheet." It now says the
  page is not filed under a category and to drag it from the category list.

2026-10-26
- A character with the Lucky descriptor now gets a Luck Pool. It sits as a
  fourth panel below the three pool cards, with a Pool box, a Max box and the
  descriptor's rule. Luck is never rolled on its own, so the panel has no d20
  button.
- The Luck Pool has a checkbox in the gear menu. Turning it off clears the Pool,
  so the checkbox asks twice before it does anything.
- The guided roller has a "Luck toward this roll" stepper above the Effort
  steppers. You take the points from Luck first, and they raise what the action
  can cost, so a level of Effort your stat Pool could not pay for on its own
  becomes available. Nothing is spent until you roll, and a line near the bottom
  shows what your Luck will be afterwards.
- The roller's cost line and the chat card both name the two halves of what an
  action costs, as in "1 Might, 1 Luck", with your Edge coming off the whole cost
  once rather than off either share. A line below the card's gives your Luck
  after the roll.
- Luck reaching 0 does not move you down the damage track. Only the three stat
  Pools do that.
- A recovery roll's card states the rule that Luck refreshes by the same number
  of points you restore to your other Pools, up to its maximum. The sheet does
  not know how you split a recovery roll, so that arithmetic stays yours.
- If your character file renames the Pool, the new name is used everywhere: the
  panel, the roller's stepper and after-line, and every chat card.
- The book's longer rules now sit behind a small arrow instead of being printed
  in full. That covers the worn armor rule, the lines about moving on the damage
  track, and the restrictions on burning a Stress level. Click the arrow to read
  them, and click it again to put them away.
- The checkbox behind the pencil that grants the Hurt step is now labelled HURT
  STEP, to match the other labels in that panel. The two things that grant the
  step, the Tough ability and the Never Says Die focus, moved into the Hurt
  step's own note. Click the word Hurt to read it.

2026-10-12
- A game that uses The Magnus Archives Stress rules now gets a Stress column in
  the Damage & Recovery panel. Click the pencil and tick Stress to turn it on.
  The column has a Points row and a Supernatural levels row. Each has a minus
  button, a box you can type in, and a plus button. Click a row's name to read
  the book's rules for that row.
- Ticking the Damage track or Stress checkbox on now asks twice as well. Before,
  only switching one off did. A character can arrive from another sheet carrying
  Stress points while the Stress rule is off, and turning the rule on there sets
  those points back to zero. So the first click turns the label red and says what
  it is about to clear, and only a second click turns the rule on. The 2026-09-30
  entry below says this happens when you switch a rule off. It now happens in
  both directions. It still asks only when that rule is holding something you
  could lose, so a checkbox with nothing behind it flips on the first click.
- The Points row also has a -3 button. Three points is one hour of rest, and
  also exactly one Stress level.
- A line under the Points row gives your Stress level and says how many steps
  your actions are hindered. Every 3 points is one level. From four levels a
  second line says each further level is also a serious injury and the character
  moves one step down the damage track. The sheet never moves the track for you.
  From ten supernatural levels a line says the character can take supernatural
  type abilities and become an Avatar.
- The Stress column has a Worn armor checkbox with the book's rule printed
  beside it. Ticking it gives the character the Hurt step on the damage track,
  and it hinders Speed tasks in the guided roller.
- The damage track now has a Hurt step above Impaired. It appears when the
  character wears armor, or when you tick the checkbox behind the pencil that
  reads "Has the Hurt step: the Tough ability, or the Never Says Die focus".
  Hurt imposes no penalties. Most characters skip it and go straight to
  Impaired.
- With the Stress rule on, the guided roller gains a Hindrances group. It holds
  a stepper for how many Stress levels hinder this roll, capped at the levels
  the character has. It holds a checkbox for worn armor hindering a Speed task,
  ticked by default. It holds a checkbox to burn a Stress level, which eases the
  roll one step, costs 3 points and lowers the hindrance cap. The GM owes you an
  intrusion for a burn, and the roller says so beside the box.
- The chat card names each of those terms, so you can see what hindered the roll
  and what the burn cost. A burn also prints your Stress points and level after
  the roll.
- An ability row can carry a Stress cost. It shows beside the ability's Pool
  cost, on the row and on the ability's chat card. The sheet records the cost and
  does not spend it for you.
- An impaired character now pays the damage track's extra Effort point per
  level. One level costs 4 instead of 3, and two cost 7 instead of 5. The chat
  card breaks the cost out, as in "5 + 2 impaired". A game with the damage track
  off is unchanged.

2026-09-30
- The sheet now reads character files that use the Old Gods of Appalachia and
  The Magnus Archives rules. Import one from Cypher Tools the same way you
  always have.
- A game that uses the damage track gets it in place of the wound boxes. The
  Damage & Recovery panel lists Hale, Impaired, Debilitated and Dead. Click the
  marker beside a step to say that is where your character is, and it fills in.
  Click the step's name instead to read what that step does to the character. A
  line under the track says how a character moves up and down it. The sheet never
  moves the step for you.
- To turn the track on or off, click the pencil on the Damage & Recovery panel
  and use the Damage track checkbox. Turning it off brings the wound boxes back
  with the values they had. It also forgets which step you were on, so turning
  the track back on starts again at Hale.
- Switching the Damage track or Stress checkbox off throws away what that rule
  was holding: the step your character was on, or your Stress points and
  supernatural levels. There is no way to get those back from the sheet, so the
  checkbox now asks twice. The first click turns the label red and tells you what
  it is about to clear, and only a second click does it. Use the other checkbox
  instead, or close the pencil, and nothing is cleared. It asks only when there is
  something to lose. A track still sitting at Hale, or Stress at zero, switches
  off on the first click, the way the bullet above describes.
- In a game that uses the damage track, a guided roll that spends Effort and
  empties a Pool now says so on the chat card, because emptying a Pool moves a
  character one step down the track. A game with the track off is unchanged and
  gets no such line.

2026-09-17
- You can drag a page out of the Cypher compendium and drop it onto the sheet.
  An ability, a skill, a cypher, an artifact or a piece of equipment lands as a
  new row in the right list, the sheet switches to the tab holding that list, and
  the new row is highlighted so you can see where it went. Drop the same page
  twice and you get two rows, because the sheet does not assume a duplicate is a
  mistake.
- A page the sheet cannot turn into a row says so instead of doing nothing. A
  focus or a type page tells you to pick it in the character's sentence. A page
  with nothing for the sheet to read says the page is malformed. A page written
  for a newer sheet says the sheet needs updating, and one written for an older
  sheet says it cannot be read.
- An NPC stat block refuses a drop, because its rows are not the character
  lists.
- A viewer who can see a character but cannot change it should not drag onto it.
  The row appears for a moment and then disappears, because the server refuses
  the change. Nothing is damaged and nothing is saved. This is a known rough
  edge and it is being fixed.

2026-09-16
- A character renamed in the Roll20 journal keeps its new name. Before, the
  next edit on the sheet could put the old name back. Portrait, bio and GM
  notes changes stick the same way.
- Importing the wrong file now says what it is. If you picked the Foundry
  export in Cypher Tools, the sheet tells you to export again with the Roll20
  option. Problems in a file are listed by field, and a refused export says
  where on the sheet to fix it.

2026-09-15
- Attacks can now be rolled. Click the d20 beside an attack to open the guided
  roller for it. Your training with the attack, a light weapon, and the
  attack's modifier ease the roll automatically, and each one is listed so you
  can see where the ease came from.
- Enter the creature's level instead of a difficulty. The chat card says Hit or
  Miss, and shows your damage only on a hit.
- You can spend Effort on damage as well as on hitting. Each level adds 3
  damage. Both kinds of Effort share one cost, so your Edge comes off once for
  the attack.
- A natural 17 or 18 adds its bonus damage to the total. On a 19 or 20 the card
  offers a choice between the special effect and extra damage. A natural 20
  refunds all the Effort you spent.

2026-09-14
- New characters now open the Settings window automatically the first time the
  sheet is opened, so you can pick a skin and your roll options right away.
  It only happens once per character. Characters you already have are not
  affected.
- GMs can turn any sheet into an NPC stat block. Open the gear menu and set
  Sheet type to NPC. The card follows the Cypher GM's Guide, with level,
  health, damage, armor, movement, combat actions, GM intrusions and more.
  Switching back to Character keeps everything the sheet had.
- An NPC's health can be linked to a token bar.
- Only GMs see an NPC's stat block. Players see a short notice instead, but
  keep NPC journal entries hidden from players, since the sheet only hides the
  view.
- GMs can export a creature to a file and import it onto another NPC sheet.
